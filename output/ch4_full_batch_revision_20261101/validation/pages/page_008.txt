To address migration externalities under batch computation sharing, a deadline￾prioritized Multi-Candidate Iterative Task Reallocation (MC-ITR) method based on
full-batch reevaluation is developed. Single-request migration is analyzed in terms of
its effects on other requests’ latencies and deadline outcomes. The method searches
single-request neighborhoods using a strict lexicographic objective prioritizing deadline
misses, normalized latency, and assignment energy, with strict descent, finite termina￾tion, and neighborhood local optimality upon non-improving termination. In the main
fixed-snapshot experiment, MC-ITR reduces mean deadline misses from 2.228 to 2.055
relative to an own-request iterative baseline sharing its initialization and neighborhood,
an improvement of approximately 7.8%, while model assignment energy and recorded
search time increase by approximately 13.7% and 30.7%, respectively. Counterfactual
allocation along historical demand-aware trajectories reduces deadline misses by 2.46
per snapshot. These results support offline allocation improvements within the specified
simulation model, without guarantees of global optimality or online closed-loop gains.
To bridge the gap between combinatorial allocation quality and strict online
decision-time budgets, a cost-aware hierarchical service decision framework is pro￾posed. Combining the demand-aware Attention-MAPPO trajectory policy in the upper
layer with a structured lower-layer scheduler, the high-dimensional offloading problem
is decomposed into node-level integer quota proposals and request-level capacity-slot
linear assignments within a strict budget of at most 10 matching calls. Furthermore, an
analytical cache-mixture scoring function is formulated to decouple cache-hit and cache￾miss deadline branches, integrated with candidate-level energy pricing and closed-loop
injection of actual software execution latency. In closed-loop evaluations with 5 UAVs
serving 100 UEs, the hierarchical method achieves a mean full-call decision latency of
only 17.72 ms (p95 = 24.69 ms) and attains a cost-aware end-to-end DSR of 33.76%,
outperforming the greedy-repair baseline and the binary-scoring priced strategy by 1.03
and 0.26 percentage points, respectively, within prespecified fleet energy constraints.
